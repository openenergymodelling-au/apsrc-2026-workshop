With the projected retirement of thermal coal power plants and the associated
increase in the penetration of variable renewable energy sources and storage in
the National Electricity Market (NEM), ensuring reliability across all
timesteps will depend strongly on the operation of storage units. In adequacy
assessments, storage behaviour is often simplified, adopting, for example,
perfect foresight of outage conditions or heuristic, adequacy-optimistic
operational approximations. However, operational experience increasingly
challenges these assumptions, highlighting a discrepancy between modelled and
real-world storage behaviour and leaving the implications of these
approximations for NEM adequacy uncertain. To support reliability planning in
the NEM, we present an open-source adequacy assessment methodology and related
tools developed as part of the Australian Research in Power Systems Transition
(AR-PST) Stage 5. We evaluate adequacy under AEMO's 2024 Integrated System Plan
assumptions, comparing different operational representations and their
implications for reliability. Results show that reliability levels of the NEM
become sensitive to storage operation approximations post-2030, and assumptions
about state of charge, charging behaviour, and dispatch incentives materially
change the estimated reliability risk.
